# Threat model

What VOIDFORGE protects, who it protects against, what it does about each threat, and what it cannot do. SECURITY.md says how VOIDFORGE is checked and how to report a problem. VOIDFORGE has not been independently audited.

## What is being protected

1. **The seed words and passphrase** (and the rolls, flips, cards or draws they came from). Anyone who has them can take the funds.
2. **The right seed.** The words, fingerprint and addresses on screen must be the ones the standards (BIP39, BIP32 and the rest) give for your inputs, so a backup made today still opens the wallet years from now.
3. **Your privacy.** Which addresses and keys are yours.

## How it is meant to be used

- The downloaded `voidforge.html`, checked with a tool that is not the page (the signature and SHA-256, see SECURITY.md), copied to a USB stick and opened on a computer that is **offline** for the whole session, ideally one used for nothing else.
- Randomness that **you** make with dice, a coin, cards or a word draw. VOIDFORGE never makes the randomness for a seed itself.
- The words written on paper or stamped in metal, and checked with a second independent tool or device before real funds go to them.
- Phones and the online copy at https://random21m.github.io/VOIDFORGE/ are for learning and practice only, never for real funds.

## Which amounts

- **Making a seed** (dice, coins, cards, the Damascus method) and **checking a backup** are meant for real savings, used as described above: offline, the file checked, and the words compared with a second independent tool or device before funds go to them.
- **Real signing in the browser** (v2.17.0) is for learning, for amounts you could afford to lose, and for getting coins out when your usual signer is lost or broken. A browser tab is not a hardware signer. For savings, sign with a hardware signer or with Bitcoin Core on a dedicated offline computer.
- These are guidance, not limits: the page does not count your coins and cannot stop you.

## What VOIDFORGE adds, and what it does not prove

Some careful setups use Bitcoin Core alone, on dedicated offline computers, and argue that any extra tool is more code that could steal or leak a key. That is a fair point, and it applies to VOIDFORGE:

- **It is more software.** VOIDFORGE runs on top of whatever wallet you use; it does not replace Bitcoin Core or a hardware signer. Its code turns your rolls into words, and a bug or a changed copy there could make a bad seed even when every roll was fair. Watching your own rolls does not prove that code is honest.
- **The release signature covers the file, not what runs it.** The signature and SHA-256 prove you have the file the maintainer released. They say nothing about the browser engine, the operating system or the computer that run it, and nothing about whether that file is correct.
- **What you can do about it:** compare the words and fingerprint VOIDFORGE gives with a second tool it did not write: the same rolls on a SeedSigner or a Coldcard (the page uses their dice method and is tested against their code), or the words in Bitcoin Core or another wallet. The file is one readable HTML page with no network code, and its source and tests are public.
- **Why make your own randomness at all.** A device's own random number generator can fail silently for years. Coinkite's own security advisory says COLDCARD firmware from 2021 to July 2026 made seeds with far fewer random bits than intended, and large thefts from weak seeds were reported after it; the page's *Was my seed made by a flawed device?* lists it with its source. Dice take the device's generator out of the seed, but make you the source of randomness and add VOIDFORGE's code as the converter. Neither choice removes trust; they move it. The check with a second tool above is what covers the converter.

## Who might attack, and what VOIDFORGE does

| Threat | What VOIDFORGE does | What is left to you |
|---|---|---|
| **A changed copy of the file** (a fake download site, a changed release, a changed USB stick) that makes predictable seeds or sends them out | Releases are signed (from v2.8.1) and list their SHA-256 and content ID. The page's own self-test cannot prove the file is genuine, and the page says so. | Check the signature and the SHA-256 with tools that are not the page, every time you copy the file. |
| **The network:** the page sending your seed somewhere | The page has no code that talks to the network, and a Content-Security-Policy built into the file tells the browser to block all network access. The NETWORK tag shows whether the computer is online. | Keep the computer offline; the policy is a second line of defense, not the first. |
| **Weak randomness:** a browser or library random number generator that is broken or backdoored | Seeds come only from your physical rolls, flips, cards or draws, hashed with SHA-256 the way SeedSigner and Coldcard do. A fairness test warns about lopsided dice. | Use real dice (casino dice are best), roll them yourself, enter every roll. The fairness test cannot prove dice are fair. |
| **Wrong math:** a bug that gives the wrong words, fingerprint or addresses | 155 known-answer checks run each time the page opens and turn the key tools off if one fails. Every release is tested against independent libraries, Bitcoin Core, the device makers' own dice code, three browser engines, and about 2,500 deliberate bugs that must all be caught. | Compare the words and fingerprint with a second independent tool or device. |
| **Other software on the computer:** browser extensions, page translators, screen recorders, malware, a hidden camera | *Is anything watching this page?* looks for the signs extensions and translators leave. Secrets cannot be selected or copied by accident, and CLEAR ALL, auto-clear and END SESSION wipe them from the page. | The page can never show that nothing is watching. Use a clean offline computer with no extensions, and close the browser when done. |
| **The camera** (v2.9.0) turned on without you knowing, or a seed QR scanned on an online computer | The camera turns on only when you press a SCAN button, shows a red CAMERA ON bar, and stops as soon as a code is read, when you press STOP CAMERA, change tabs or leave the page, and after 2 minutes. Pictures are read inside the page and never saved or sent. A SeedQR is scanned only while the page sees no network. | Never scan a real seed QR with a phone or an online device. Cover or unplug the camera when you are not scanning. |
| **A seed that is weak or public without you knowing** (v2.10.0): a test phrase, a phrase typed from an example, a seed made by a device with a known randomness flaw | A public example phrase is named after FORGE SEED, in the CHECK A BACKUP words box, VERIFY, Open this wallet, the Wallet Detective and Seed XOR (a warning, never a block). *Was my seed made by a flawed device?* lists only confirmed flaws, each with its source, and never calls a seed safe. *Check a Coldcard seed mix* shows whether a Coldcard mixed your dice in as its code says. | "No known flaw" is not a guarantee. The TRNG words and rolls typed into the seed-mix check are the seed: use it offline only. |
| **Backups that show more than you meant** (v2.10.0) | Punch plates, word numbers, old Electrum hex seeds and SLIP39 shares are as secret as the words: marked SECRET, not selectable, printed only after a question, cleared with the words. Inheritance papers print no words or passphrase. A .bip138 backup holds no private keys. | A .bip138 file opens with any one of the wallet's xpubs, so anyone who has seen one (a coordinator, a server) can read the descriptor. The key details sheet lets its reader see every address and the balance. Someone with your SLIP39 dice rolls can make the shares again: keep the roll sheet as secret as the shares. |
| **Device backups opened here** (v2.12.0) | A TAPSIGNER, Krux or Coldcard backup is opened only in the page, offline; the words or key are shown only when asked, in a box that cannot be selected, and CLEAR, CLEAR ALL and auto-clear wipe them. A wrong key is told apart by each format's own check (a Base58Check xprv, Krux's hash or GCM tag, Coldcard's CRC32); older Krux seeds.json entries have none, so only the words' own checksum tells. Krux's IDs are compared with the words' fingerprint, and a Coldcard's own xprv line with its seed. | Opening a backup puts its private keys in this browser's memory: do it only on the offline computer. A TAPSIGNER whose card-back key has been used is no longer secure (Coinkite); a Krux check of 3 or 4 bytes can, very rarely, let a wrong key through, so compare the fingerprint. |
| **The Practice signer** (v2.13.0): about 1 MB of new code, and a real seed in it | It runs only in the page, with no network code, so it is as safe as the page itself, and it says so under the screen. It is a real, working signer: like SeedSigner, it signs any transaction with any seed typed, scanned or loaded into it (it starts on Mainnet, like SeedSigner); since v2.16.1 it takes any seed on purpose, as the real device does. Re-hide covers it while it holds a seed that is not public, like the page's other secrets. Its own camera reads a real seed's QR only while the page sees no network, the same rule as the SCAN buttons; since v2.17.0 a public seed's QR is read online too, and LOAD INTO THE PRACTICE SIGNER loads a real seed online only after a plain warning and a yes. CLEAR ALL, auto-clear, END SESSION and closing its box wipe its seeds, keys, passphrases, dice rolls, transactions and signatures (closing the box keeps only the settings saved to its practice microSD, and a later auto-clear, CLEAR ALL or END SESSION wipes those too); it saves nothing in the browser, so its settings are gone when the page closes. It reads animated UR (fountain code) QR codes, and it leaves out Taproot script-path signing. The drawing is a molded case in VOIDFORGE colors with no USB cable. Its code is tested against independent libraries and not run on any real device. | Use it with a real seed only on an offline computer, as the page itself: it is a port, and a screen or rule may differ from the real SeedSigner. Wiping the page does not erase everything the browser held: restart the computer after a session. Its code is a large part of the file, and it has not been independently audited. |
| **A seed made by hand, the Damascus method** (v2.14.0): a copying mistake, one bad source of randomness, a helper who steers the seed | The seed is dice folded with cards (same bits give 0, different bits give 1), so it is random if either source is good. Hallmarks, the seal and the word check catch copying mistakes, and CHECK MY SHEETS points to the row and column of one. The dice and card checks catch badly broken dice or a lazy shuffle. The last word is worked out by two tools that must agree. **Practice sheets come from a published rule** (SHA-256 of a fixed text), not from the page's random numbers (the page makes none), so anyone can make the same sheet: every result says PRACTICE, and FORGE SEED (since v2.16.1) shows it under YOUR SEED with a red PRACTICE banner, and every sheet printed and file saved from it says PRACTICE. **The assay reads one sheet only** and shows that sheet's hallmarks and its own Seed XOR phrase, never the Forge grid or the seed. CLEAR, CLEAR ALL, auto-clear and END SESSION wipe the boxes and results. | Hallmarks catch mistakes, not cheating. The checks cannot prove dice or a shuffle fair: small flaws pass. **Hallmarks reveal part of the seed** (22 bits for 12 words, 34 for 24): keep them only with the words, never on their own. Anyone who sees the Hammer sheet before filling in the Anvil sheet can choose the seed, and every check still passes (worked example 2): fill each sheet in private and fold it closed. An assayer's phrase is a Seed XOR part: keep it as secret as the words. Doing this by hand takes care, and the method has not been independently audited. |
| **Lessons on the practice signer** (v2.14.0): a practice seed or transaction mistaken for a real one | With no seed on the device, a lesson loads the **public test seed** abandon ... about (anyone can load it, and the lessons say never to put coins on it); since v2.17.0 the lessons use the seed on the device, real or practice, and name it by its fingerprint. **The practice transaction is made-up coins:** its earlier transaction is invented and marked as practice, so it can never be broadcast, and the page says so. FORGE SEED below the device marks its result PRACTICE when it is public; the fingerprint line it shows next to the device's is a consistency check only, not an independent one. LOAD INTO THE PRACTICE SIGNER (since v2.16.1 any seed, in one click) holds the seed's SeedQR up to the device and presses its keys; it puts nothing into the device's memory itself. A public or practice seed loads online or offline; a real seed loads online only after a plain warning and a yes (v2.17.0). It presses Scan only while the SeedQR is held up, so it never opens the computer's camera. Wiping the page's seed (CLEAR ALL, ADD THIS KEY & WIPE THE SEED) also wipes the practice signer's copy. The lessons only watch which screen the device shows; nothing is drawn on its screen or added to its menus. While the practice wallet holds up a QR code, the lesson's scan reads that and does not open the computer's camera. CLEAR ALL, auto-clear, END SESSION and closing the box drop the lesson, the practice wallet's QR codes and the practice transaction, and overwrite the transaction's bytes. | Never fund a seed from a lesson: its seeds are public. A lesson is a guide to a port: the real SeedSigner may word or order a screen differently. The same limits as the practice signer above apply. |
| **Real signing with the practice signer** (v2.17.0): your own PSBT or message signed with a real seed in the browser | The device reviews the PSBT as SeedSigner does and signs exactly as SeedSigner does (checked byte for byte against independent libraries for every script type). Before the scan the page warns about the wrong network (never signed), a PSBT no loaded seed can sign, inputs of other signers or left unsigned, sighash types SeedSigner does not sign, high fees (red above the payment or 1,000 sat/vB, amber at 10% of the amount sent or 250 sat/vB), change at an odd path, multisig change that only the PSBT claims ("NOT verified", in red), and outputs that name the seed but are not provably its own (shown as payments). The device refuses a PSBT whose full previous transaction is not the coin it spends or disagrees with its witness UTXO (a forged amount that hides a fee; SeedSigner 0.8.7 does not check this). A descriptor counts only if it holds the seed's own key and makes the coins spent. Before a seed that is not public signs a PSBT or a message, the page asks you to confirm the outputs are checked and the computer is offline, with a stronger warning while it is online, and a second time for unverified multisig change; No signs nothing. The signed PSBT is offered only when the page's own code finds it is the transaction held up (sequences kept exactly) and every signature valid. Seeds and keys in their common forms are kept out of the box (a safety net, not a guarantee). A public seed signs as PRACTICE. Re-hide covers the signed output while a real seed is loaded and every message signature; every wipe clears them and the typed message. Taproot signatures use no aux randomness (as SeedSigner's libsecp256k1 does with none given). | **A browser is not a hardware signer:** anything watching the computer (malware, an extension, a screen recorder, a clipboard tool) can copy a seed loaded here, and a yes to the online question is your own choice. Use it with real coins only on an offline computer, wipe the seed and restart afterwards. The page cannot tell what you meant to pay: check every address and amount on the device's screen against your wallet. It is a port, not SeedSigner itself, and it has not been independently audited. The SegWit v0 two-pass fee attack (a wallet asks you to sign the same transaction twice, lowering a different input's amount each time, and combines the signatures) cannot be seen in one PSBT: it works against SeedSigner 0.8.7 and this page; an amber note shows when a multi-input PSBT leaves out a SegWit v0 input's full previous transaction. Be wary of signing the same transaction again; prefer PSBTs with full previous transactions, which the page checks. |
| **Secrets left on screen** (v2.15.0): someone glances at the screen after you look away, or a printout of the page shows words | Re-hide covers secrets again when the tab is hidden, the window loses focus, the browser's own Print runs, or the page is idle (5 minutes by default; off, 1, 2 or 5). SHOW uncovers them; nothing is wiped. VOIDFORGE's own PRINT sheets are not affected. Since v2.16.1 the practice signer is covered while it holds a seed that is not public, a seed is being loaded or a passphrase is typed on it. | It hides the screen only. It does **not** protect against malware, a screen recorder already running, a keylogger, or someone watching while the secrets are shown. Use an offline, clean computer. |
| **A passphrase typed wrong or unusable on a device** (v2.15.0) | The passphrase check under each passphrase box shows spaces and line breaks as marks, warns when NFKD changes the text, when it is not plain ASCII, and above 60 and above 100 characters. Sources: BIP39 (NFKD), the Coldcard firmware's passphrase screen (100 characters, ASCII 32 to 126) and SeedSigner 0.8.7's keyboard (printable ASCII). Practice your passphrase compares two typings on a public test seed. | A passphrase that passes the check can still be the wrong one: any passphrase opens some wallet. Write it down exactly. |
| **A QR code that shows a secret** (v2.15.0): Link as QR | It makes a QR code from a link or public text only, and refuses common secret formats: seed words, SeedQR digits, codex32, hex keys, WIF, mini keys and xprv. | A safety net, not a guarantee: a secret in a format it does not know would be drawn. Never put anything secret in it. |
| **An old wallet file opened on a risky computer** (v2.16.0): Open an Electrum wallet file, Read a Bitcoin Core wallet file | Both only read: offline, nothing is sent and the file is never written. The seed and private keys are behind SHOW THE SECRET and SHOW THE PRIVATE KEYS, are covered again by re-hide, and are wiped by CLEAR, CLEAR ALL, auto-clear and END SESSION. Addresses are checked against the file (Electrum) or worked out from its keys (Bitcoin Core). | A wallet file holds private keys, so open a copy on an offline computer. A malicious or damaged file is refused with a reason, but a computer that is compromised sees what you open. The network of a legacy wallet.dat is a guess. Built on Electrum 4.8.4 (commit b991f249ded4) and Bitcoin Core v29.0 source files; hardware-encrypted Electrum files (BIE2) are refused. |
| **New share and multisig formats** (v2.11.0) | Codex32 shares get every random value from your dice, through a stream labeled with all the settings, so two sets never share a random value; they are SECRET, never copyable, and printed only after a question. A codex32 checksum catches mistakes, not tampering, so the page shows the fingerprint to compare. Taproot multisig has no spendable key path. | Taproot multisig keys use a path BIP48 does not define (m/48'/coin'/account'/3'), so keep the descriptor backup: tools that only know BIP48 will not find the wallet. Use new rolls for codex32 shares, never the rolls of a seed you already use. |
| **What stays behind:** browser memory, the clipboard, print spoolers, swap files | Wiping is tested: after CLEAR ALL a memory snapshot holds no words, rolls or passphrases. The page stores nothing on disk except the theme, text size and a once-per-tab notice flag. The seed words have no copy button, and the page warns that clipboard history can keep what you paste. | A browser cannot erase everything it ever held. Restart the computer after a session; prefer writing words by hand over printing or copying them. |
| **Someone who sees the backup:** a burglar, a visitor, a family member | Explains passphrases, multisig, decoy wallets, Seed XOR and how to store backups; a backup plan and stress test help you think it through. | Where the backups live is up to you. |
| **A forged transaction or message** shown to you for checking | The transaction inspector and the signed-message check are read-only; they never sign. A change mark in a transaction is checked against the wallet's own keys when you type the words, and called unchecked otherwise. | Check the receiving address on your signing device's own screen. |
| **Scams aimed at heirs and beginners** | Heir mode explains backups calmly and lists common scams. VOIDFORGE never asks for words online and has no support chat. | Nobody genuine will ever ask for your words. |

## Not protected against

- A computer that is compromised at the hardware or firmware level, or a browser that is itself malicious.
- Someone who gets your written words or passphrase.
- A signer and the wallet that builds its transactions working together to leak a key in its signatures (a chosen nonce, "Dark Skippy", 2024). VOIDFORGE signs with fixed nonces (RFC 6979; BIP-340 with no aux randomness), so the same transaction signed again elsewhere with the same seed gives the same signatures, but the page has no anti-exfil protocol and cannot check a signature made by another device without the seed.
- Timing attacks on the key math: the secp256k1 code is not constant-time, which is acceptable only because the computer is offline and not shared.
- Mistakes the page cannot see: a word written down wrongly, a roll left out, a passphrase forgotten. The restore drill and VERIFY help catch these before funds are sent.

## Trust you still place

- In the browser engine, the operating system and the hardware of the offline computer. The release signature does not cover them.
- In VOIDFORGE's own code, unless you check its results with a second tool it did not write.
- In the person who signs releases (one maintainer key; see SECURITY.md for its fingerprint).
- In the standards themselves (BIP39, BIP32 and the rest) and in the independent libraries the tests compare against.
