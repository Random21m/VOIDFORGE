# VOIDFORGE

An offline, educational tool that turns dice rolls or coin flips into a BIP39 seed phrase (12 or 24 words), then helps you back it up and set up a wallet, including multisig (a simple slider picks 2 to 15 keys). One HTML file, no dependencies, no network.

## Try it online (practice only)

You can try VOIDFORGE without downloading anything, on a computer or a phone: **[random21m.github.io/VOIDFORGE](https://random21m.github.io/VOIDFORGE/)**. It is meant for seeing whether you like it, learning how dice become words, and practising with a seed you will never use.

- **It is the same file as the release.** The online copy serves the latest released `voidforge.html` byte for byte, inside a thin page that adds the amber banner. No server code, no account, no analytics and nothing stored. The file's own rule (a Content-Security-Policy) still blocks all network access, so the page cannot send what you type anywhere.
- **It still warns you.** Besides the banner, the app shows its red *NETWORK: ONLINE* warning, because a connected device is the wrong place for real keys. The banner has an *Online or downloaded?* note that sets out the difference.
- **Never for real money.** A connected phone or computer runs other apps, browser extensions and sync services that can see or keep what you type. For real funds download the file from the [latest release](../../releases/latest), check its SHA-256 (the *Check your download* tool in the app does this) and open it on an offline computer.
- **Only trust this address.** A copy of the page on any other site could be altered. GitHub can see that you loaded the page, though the page itself sends nothing.
- **Add it to your Home Screen** from your browser's share or menu button, if you want a shortcut to the practice copy.

## Quick start

1. Download `voidforge.html` from the [latest release](../../releases/latest) and check it: `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`.
2. Copy it to a USB stick and open it in a desktop browser (Safari 14+, Chrome or Firefox) on an **offline computer**. A phone is fine for learning and practice (the page works in a phone browser once you open it from a real file, not a preview, or from the [online practice copy](https://random21m.github.io/VOIDFORGE/)), but never for real funds: use an offline computer for those.
3. Confirm the green **SELF-TEST PASSED (81/81)** badge and the **NETWORK: OFFLINE** tag. A red notice saying the script is not running means the page did not start; do not use it.
4. Pick Dice, Coin or Word draw, choose 12 or 24 words, enter your physical rolls or flips (type, paste, or tap the buttons; the minimum or more, and every one counts) and watch the grey preview words change with every roll, then press FORGE SEED. The words appear as a numbered grid. A passphrase, if you want one, goes in the box right under the words ("Passphrase and note"); changing it does not clear your words, it only works out the fingerprint and addresses again.
5. Write the words down, then compare the words and the master fingerprint with a second independent offline tool before trusting real funds.

**Layout (v2.1.0, still current in v2.4.0):** one narrow column, the same on a phone and a desktop. The header has the golden die and the name VOIDFORGE on the left, with DOWNLOAD FOR USB and a MENU on the right (GUIDE ME, TEXT A+, PRINT SEED, PRINT STAMP SHEET, the light/dark switch and, last and in red, END SESSION live in the MENU). A hairline runs across the page under the header, and four file-style tabs sit below it: *Make a seed*, *Check a backup*, *Extra tools* and *About & help*. Inside *Make a seed* a step bar shows where you are (choose, roll, forge, passphrase, check), a short line under it says what the step is for and why, three tabs pick how you make the seed (Dice, Coin or Word draw), the roll counter is red until you have enough rolls or flips and then green with a tick, and your seed words appear right below the FORGE SEED button, inside their boxes at every screen width. The page opens in charcoal; the menu switches to white. Red is used only for things that warn or wipe.

**How this differs from other offline tools.** Tools such as Ian Coleman's BIP39 page and bitaddress.org are well known and useful, and VOIDFORGE is not a replacement for them. The difference is the focus: VOIDFORGE is built for one job, making a wallet seed from dice or coins you roll yourself, with the words and the checks explained in plain language. Its entropy is only the rolls or flips you type in (never mouse movement, timing or the browser's random generator), and it has no paper-wallet or single-key mode. Using a second tool to compare your words and fingerprint is still a good idea, and the page tells you to.

**Fairness test and Word finder (v1.25.1):** under the roll chart, once you have 30 rolls (20 flips) the page shows a Pearson chi-square number and p-value for the face counts, with a plain-language reading. It can show that counts look uneven; it can never prove your dice or coin are fair. In the Word draw box, a Word finder turns a word number (1 to 2048), a D8 roll plus two hex dice, or (v2.3.0) a BitBox02 diceware code such as `2143 2 T` into the BIP39 word, so dice-to-word methods can be followed by hand; ADD WORD (or Enter) puts it at the end of your words.

**BIP85 outputs other than seed words are advanced and secret.** The Child seed tool also makes a Bitcoin Core HD seed (WIF), a master xprv, raw hex, Base64 and Base85 passwords and dice rolls, from the same 64 bytes of BIP85 entropy. They are live secrets: each one is shown on screen only, with no copy or save (since v2.4.0 a print button prints it on one sheet, after asking), and is matched against the published BIP85 test vectors and independent code.

**Not checkable here: signer anti-exfil (sign-to-contract).** Hardware wallets such as Jade can prove their signing nonce was not chosen to leak your key, but the proof needs the random data the computer sent and the signer's first nonce commitment. Those exist only during the live exchange and are not stored in a PSBT or transaction, so VOIDFORGE cannot check them afterwards. The signature checks it does perform (nonce reuse, sighash types) work on the finished transaction only.

**Browser testing:** automated tests run in Chromium. v1.7.0, v1.8.0, v1.8.1, v1.9.0, v1.10.0, v1.10.1 and v1.11.0 were checked by hand in Firefox and Safari on a desktop computer (self-test badge, dice buttons, watch-only box, QR codes and, in v1.8.0, the fingerprint picture all worked; v1.5.0 also matched the published dice example). v1.7.1 only added disclaimer text, and v1.8.1 only changed one message and the release process. v1.9.0 added the roll chart. v1.10.0 added the five-address list, the 4-letter view and the check guide. The watch-only QR was scanned into Blue Wallet on a phone and worked (same first receiving address as VOIDFORGE; a zpub import shows no master fingerprint by design, while the descriptor does). The descriptors were imported into Sparrow Wallet, and the fingerprint picture matched what Sparrow shows for four wallets. The maintainer reports that later releases, up to v1.18.0, also work in Firefox and Safari on a desktop computer (reported by hand, without a per-version checklist). Electrum and Nunchuk have not been tested. Please report anything odd in other browsers. The page needs Safari 14 or newer. Phones are not supported.

**Coin flips:** decide which side is 1 and which is 0 (the usual choice is heads = 1, tails = 0, and the two tap buttons are drawn as gold coins that way), use it for every flip, and keep the exact 1/0 string with your backup. Only the string is hashed, so any consistent choice works, but the mapping cannot be recovered from the words.

- **Restore drill:** after FORGE, a card in the Check step hides the words on screen and has you type them back from your paper. It reports which word positions are wrong (never what the right word is), checks the passphrase if there is one, and shows the master fingerprint your paper rebuilds. Changing or clearing the seed ends the drill, and nothing is stored.
- **First 5 addresses and a check guide:** after FORGE the first five receiving addresses are listed (with COPY ALL) and a short guide explains how to compare them in a second wallet app. Comparing five addresses is much stronger than comparing one.
- **First 4 letters view:** a button on the word grid shows only the first four letters of each word (every BIP39 word is unique by them), for stamping metal backups. Display only; it does not change the stored words.
- **Roll chart:** a small bar chart under the roll box shows how many times each face (or coin side) has come up. It is for your eyes only and does not change the seed or the randomness check, which still cannot prove your dice or coin are fair.
- **Taproot addresses:** the first five Taproot (BIP86, `bc1p...`) receiving addresses are also listed, for checking in wallets that use Taproot. The zpub, descriptors and QR codes still describe the SegWit wallet.
- **Printable backup sheet:** PRINT SEED gives a two-column numbered sheet with the fingerprint, first address and space for notes (the passphrase is never printed).
- **Bitcoin dictionary:** a searchable, offline glossary of about 260 Bitcoin terms in plain words (keys and seeds, wallets, transactions, mining, Lightning, privacy and more), with topic buttons. It is updated with every release, and where a term appears in VOIDFORGE the entry says where.
- **Check your download:** pick the downloaded file and the page works out its SHA-256 and content ID offline, then compares them with the published ones you paste. A page cannot vouch for itself, so for real money also check with `sha256sum` (the page says so).
- **Compare a key or descriptor:** in CHECK YOUR BACKUP the backup box accepts a zpub/xpub, a wpkh descriptor or a Taproot address from a wallet app and says MATCH or NO MATCH against your words (public keys only; it cannot show the words).
- **Is this address yours?** Paste a bc1q or bc1p address; VOIDFORGE searches the receive and change chains of your words (and passphrase) and tells you the address number. Searches 50 per chain per press.
- **SeedQR input:** the words box in CHECK YOUR BACKUP also reads a SeedSigner SeedQR digit string or CompactSeedQR hex and shows the words (typed in; no camera).
- **Child seed (BIP85):** derives a new seed phrase from your parent words and passphrase (12, 18 or 24 words, any index) for a separate wallet, or any other BIP85 output: a Bitcoin Core HD seed (WIF), a master xprv, hex bytes, a Base64 or Base85 password, or dice rolls. Shown on screen, with a print button (v2.4.0) and no copy or save. The parent, the type, its settings and the index recreate it; each output matches the published BIP85 test vectors.
- **Fingerprint picture:** a small LifeHash picture next to the master fingerprint (the same one Sparrow Wallet shows), for a quick visual match.
- **Reforge (seed recovery):** in EXTRA TOOLS. *Missing or wrong words:* type your words with `?` for any you lack, and Reforge fills in one or two unknown words, finds one wrong word or two swapped words, or puts back a word missing from an unknown place. *Passphrase:* type your words and your passphrase guesses or pieces, and it tries them alone and joined, in four letter cases, optionally with one typo. Every candidate is checked against something public you still have from the wallet (its master fingerprint, an address, or its account xpub/zpub), and it reports only what matched, says plainly when nothing did, and says how likely a fingerprint-only match is to be a coincidence. Typing 11 words and `?` lists the possible last words (this replaces Find the last word). What you type is secret: it stays in the page and CLEAR wipes it. A browser can only do small searches; for bigger ones, open-source tools such as BTCRecover run on a computer you trust.
- **Transaction (PSBT) inspector:** paste or choose a partly signed Bitcoin transaction (or a raw one) to see its inputs, outputs, addresses, fee, whether it can be replaced (RBF), any locktime and any data note before you sign it on a hardware wallet. It also reads any signatures already in it and flags a **repeated nonce** (two different signatures sharing a secret number, which would let anyone work out the private key) and any **sighash type other than ALL** (which lets others change the transaction after it is signed). It ends with a list of checks and a verdict, and says **NO CLEAN VERDICT** whenever it cannot fully check something. Read only: it never signs and never uses your seed.
- **Compare two transactions:** paste what you sent to your signer and what came back, or an original and its fee-bump replacement. It shows every difference in plain words, tells you whether the signer changed anything, and whether a replacement is a plain fee bump (same payments, more fee). It also checks for a repeated nonce across the two transactions. Read only.
- **Testnet and multisig cosigner keys:** a Network menu switches the watch-only export and the address finder to testnet, and the Multisig cosigner types give the BIP48 key and origin for a multisig wallet (Sparrow, Specter, Nunchuk, Electrum). Public keys only.
- **Address types and accounts:** the watch-only export can be switched to Taproot, Nested SegWit (ypub) or Legacy, with any account number, and "Is this address yours?" finds bc1q, bc1p, 3... and 1... addresses for the chosen account. Since v1.26.0 the box shows the first five receiving addresses for the chosen type, account and network, with COPY ALL, so you can compare them in your wallet app. Your 12 or 24 words do not depend on the address type: the type is chosen in the wallet app, so write it down (and the account number) with your backup.
- **Paper backup as a SeedQR:** after FORGE (12 or 24 words), SHOW SEEDQR or SHOW COMPACTSEEDQR displays the code on screen. HAND-COPY GUIDE walks through it in 7x7 blocks with row/column numbers and a mini-map so you can copy it with a pen or pencil onto grid paper, like SeedSigner. You can also print the QR, the guide or a blank grid (printing asks for confirmation, since it sends your seed to a printer). Verify a copy by scanning it into a SeedSigner and comparing the fingerprint.
- **How your seed was made:** after FORGE, an optional SECRET-marked view walks through five steps from your rolls to the words (SHA-256, raw entropy, checksum, 11-bit pieces, word numbers) and checks itself against the real words.
- **Printed backup sheet:** PRINT SEED makes a clean sheet with numbered boxes for the words, the fingerprint, first address and fields to fill in (never the passphrase).
- **Browser safety checklist:** in ABOUT & DOCS, eight short habits with the reason for each (go offline first, a clean browser window, translation, clipboard history, print and PDF copies, downloads, restarting afterwards).
- **Translation and passphrase notes (v1.29.0):** the page asks browsers not to translate it, because a translated seed word is a wrong seed word; if your browser offers to translate anyway, say no. The passphrase help also says that some hardware wallets limit how long a passphrase can be or which characters it can use, so test a small amount on your device first.
- **Stale results (v1.26.1, v1.29.1):** if you change the rolls, word count, dice/coin choice or passphrase after FORGE, the old words, fingerprint and address are cleared and you are asked to press FORGE SEED again, and DOWNLOAD saves the rolls that made the words. Since v1.29.1 a FORGE that is still working out its fingerprint and addresses is also stopped by any such change or by CLEAR ALL, so it cannot write them back late.
- **The look (v2.0.0, tidied in v2.1.0):** flat charcoal and gold, with no glows, no tumbling dice and no animation. Gold is used only for the logo, the name and the main action button on a card; helper buttons are plain outlines and CLEAR buttons have a red outline. The small labels (RECOMMENDED, SECRET, PUBLIC and the grey ones) are plain coloured words that always keep their word, so colour is never the only signal. Every text colour was checked for contrast (at least 4.5 to 1) in both themes. Seldom-used tools (Word draw details, the roll history and fairness chart, auto-clear) sit in closed boxes so the first screen stays calm. The owner's picture and the rolling-dice logo from earlier versions are gone, and the file no longer contains any picture.
- **Guide me, plain-word tips, larger text:** the GUIDE ME button (in the MENU, top right) walks a first-time user through making a seed one screen at a time (choose, roll, optional passphrase, FORGE and write down, check and back up). Each step says why, and NEXT stays locked until the step is done. Underlined words (passphrase, fingerprint, zpub, descriptor, watch-only) explain themselves on hover, tap or keyboard focus. TEXT A+ enlarges everything. These are display helpers only: they do not change any seed, key or address.
- **Live preview (v2.1.0):** while you roll, the grey word boxes show the words your rolls so far would make, using the same SHA-256 step as FORGE SEED, so you can watch one new roll change every word. They are labelled as a preview, are not your seed until you press FORGE SEED, and vanish on CLEAR. This replaces the earlier "watch the words change (demo only)" switch, which is gone.
- **Open this wallet (v2.1.0):** in *Check a backup*, once VERIFY accepts a 12, 15, 18, 21 or 24 word phrase (with or without a passphrase), a closed box *Open this wallet* lists its addresses: Native SegWit, Taproot, Nested SegWit or Legacy, mainnet or testnet, any account number, receive or change, ten at a time, with the master fingerprint and its LifeHash picture. It says plainly that the words were imported, not made here. Every address row has a small QR button (also under the seed in *Make a seed*). For 12 and 24 words it also offers SeedQR and CompactSeedQR (marked SECRET); for 15, 18 and 21 words it says that no SeedQR format exists. PRINT RECOVERY SHEET prints a sheet with no words and no passphrase: the fingerprint, address type, path and first addresses, and how to restore. Words are only ever kept in the page while the box is open, and editing the words, CLEAR ALL or a bad phrase wipes it. Generating a seed is still only for 12 or 24 words.
- **Watch-only view, Electrum warning, payment requests, checklist and key-reuse check (v2.2.0):** in *Check a backup*, *Or open a watch-only wallet* takes an account public key (xpub, ypub, zpub, or tpub, upub, vpub) or a single-key descriptor and shows the same address list with QR codes, from public keys only; a plain xpub has no master fingerprint, so the page shows one only when a descriptor gives it. If a phrase fails the BIP39 check, VERIFY also tells you when it is an Electrum seed (and which type), so you restore it in Electrum instead. Under any address QR code, *Payment request (BIP-21)* adds an amount, label or message and turns the QR into a `bitcoin:` link that phone wallets read. After FORGE, a *Backup checklist* lists four steps (words written, restore drill passed, second tool matched, recovery sheet printed); the drill and the recovery sheet tick themselves, and nothing is saved. In *Extra tools*, *Check descriptors for a reused key* warns when the same key appears twice, when two keys of one multisig come from the same seed, or when one fingerprint and path are given with two different keys. Private keys are refused everywhere.
- **More rolls, Keystone and BitBox02 dice, signed messages and END SESSION (v2.3.0):** FORGE now takes the minimum number of rolls or flips *or more*, and every one is hashed. A note under the counter says which tools make the same words: exactly 50 (12 words) or 99 (24 words) rolls match SeedSigner and Coldcard; more match Coldcard, whose dice seeds hash every roll; more than twice the minimum adds a gentle reminder to check for a double paste. PRINT ROLL SHEET has 10 extra dashed boxes. A *Dice rules* menu next to the seed length offers **Keystone**: each 6 is written as 0 before hashing, and Keystone asks for 50 rolls (12 words) or 100 (24 words), at most 256, as its firmware does. **BitBox02 diceware** works in Word draw: type each table code (four dice, a fifth die, H or T) in the Word finder and press ADD WORD, then the 3 coin flips for the last word with T as 1 and H as 0, and the 24th word is the one BitBox shows (checked against BitBox's own test vectors). **Verify a signed message** (EXTRA TOOLS, read only) checks the classic format (BIP-137, from Bitcoin Core, Electrum, Sparrow, Trezor and Coldcard) for 1..., 3... and bc1q addresses, and BIP-322 *simple* signatures for single-key bc1q and bc1p addresses, against every official BIP-322 vector and Bitcoin Core's test vector; VALID always names the address it checked, and a match found only after a small change to the message (line breaks, spaces at the ends) shows in amber with a warning. A whole signed-message block can be pasted in, but it can never replace an address you typed. It never signs. Anything it cannot check (multisig, script paths, BIP-322 *full*) says CANNOT CHECK, never VALID. Word draw seeds are now cleared if you change their words, flips or length afterwards, as dice seeds already were. **END SESSION** (MENU) wipes every box, forgets the start notice and reloads the page. The start notice now shows once in each browser tab while the computer is offline (a single "1" in this tab's session storage, kept only as long as the tab) and on every load while it is online, when nothing is stored.
- **A print for everything, one page each, and plain-language helpers (v2.4.0):** every printed sheet now comes out on **exactly one page**, on US Letter and on A4 (before, most sheets also printed blank pages); `tests/print.test.mjs` prints every sheet in a real browser to check this. A menu next to PRINT ROLL SHEET chooses **no extra boxes, 10, 25 or 50** (with Keystone dice rules, up to Keystone's maximum of 256). In *Check a backup*, once VERIFY accepts your words, *Open this wallet* has **SHOW FIRST 4 LETTERS, PRINT WORDS, PRINT STAMP SHEET** and the three SeedQR prints, for any length from 12 to 24 words; VERIFY also reads 4-letter forms (as on a metal plate) and names the **closest BIP39 words** for a word it does not know. New prints: **PRINT THIS QR** for the watch-only key and descriptors, the Silent Payment address, and address and payment-request QR codes; **PRINT CHILD SEED** (or the key or password) for BIP85; a blank **Word draw sheet**; and a **seed summary sheet** with no words on it (how the seed was made, the fingerprint and its picture, the first address), also shown as one line on the backup checklist, where printing it ticks the fourth step. A **Test only, no seed** switch in *Your rolls* turns FORGE off so you can roll a die many times just to see whether it is fair. With a tap button focused, the number keys add rolls and Backspace undoes. **Explain an address** (*Check a backup*) says what any pasted address is, which network, and whether it has a typo, with links into the dictionary; private keys, xpubs, seed words, descriptors and Lightning invoices pasted by mistake are called out. The dictionary has a short guide, **What can someone do with this?**, for seed words, passphrases, SeedQR, keys, xpubs, fingerprints, addresses and signed messages. CLEAR ALL now also clears a child seed and the address box. Seed words, fingerprints, addresses, keys and descriptors are unchanged.
- **Account finder, roll sheet, stamp sheet and auto-clear (v2.1.0):** *Is this address yours?* has TRY ACCOUNTS 0 TO 20, which looks for an address in every account (receive and change, with and without the passphrase you typed) and says which account to use. PRINT ROLL SHEET prints a blank numbered sheet for writing down dice or coin results. PRINT STAMP SHEET (MENU) prints each word as its first four letters in capitals for metal plates. *Auto-clear* is off whenever the page loads; if you turn it on, the page wipes the seed, rolls, passphrase and results after the idle time you pick, or when you leave the tab. It stores nothing.
- **Multisig setup (M-of-N):** the MULTISIG WALLET card guides you through a 2-of-3 (or any M-of-N up to 7 keys) one key at a time. Make each seed with the normal tools, back it up, then ADD THIS KEY: only the public key is kept and the seed is wiped from the page. Public keys from other devices can be pasted in (private keys, seed words, duplicates, wrong network or wrong path are refused). The result is a `wsh(sortedmulti(...))` or `sh(wsh(sortedmulti(...)))` descriptor as text, QR code and a printable recovery sheet with no seeds, plus the first addresses to compare in your wallet. Making all keys on one device works but is warned against.
- **Bitcoin Core import and Silent Payment address:** in the Watch-only box, ready-to-copy `createwallet` and `importdescriptors` commands for a watch-only Bitcoin Core wallet (with a clear choice of scanning from now or from the beginning), and a BIP-352 Silent Payment address (`sp1q...`, testnet `tsp1q...`) with a limited-wallet-support warning. A loud TESTNET banner appears whenever Testnet is chosen.
- **Word draw (physical word tiles):** if you pick your words by hand from tiles (such as SeedSigner's open-source SeedPills), type the 11 (or 23) words you drew, with the first four letters of each being enough, and the 7 (or 3) coin flips. VOIDFORGE works out the last word with the standard BIP39 checksum and shows each step. Nothing is hashed, repeated words are accepted because every tile goes back in the bag, and the flips are never filled in for you. The result works in any BIP39 wallet and uses the same fingerprint, address, SeedQR, print and watch-only tools as the dice method.
- **Watch-only export:** after FORGE, copy the wallet's public key (zpub) or descriptor into a watch-only wallet app to see addresses and balance without the seed. Public keys only; it cannot spend. SHOW QR draws it as a QR code on screen for scanning.
- Shows the master fingerprint **and the first native SegWit (BIP84) receive address** so you can cross-check with a wallet; asks you to type any passphrase twice; tap buttons for dice 1-6 and coin 1/0. VERIFY can check a phrase with and without a passphrase and tell you whether a fingerprint or address from your backup matches with a passphrase, without one, or neither.
- Guides: [RECOVERY.md](RECOVERY.md) (what to back up, how to restore) and [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) (reporting, known limits).

Reported by MrHodlX, fixed in v1.8.1; every later release uses the hardened workflow.
- Method: `SHA256(rolls or flips as text)` → BIP39. Matches SeedSigner's published dice examples; coin mode uses SeedSigner's method (1/0 string). Exactly 50 or 99 rolls give the same words as SeedSigner and Coldcard; more rolls (every one is hashed) give the same words as Coldcard's dice seeds (New Seed Words, Advanced). With the Keystone dice rules each 6 is written as 0 first, as Keystone does.
