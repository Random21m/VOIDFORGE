# VOIDFORGE

An offline, educational tool that turns dice rolls or coin flips into a BIP39 seed phrase (12, 15, 18, 21 or 24 words), then helps you back it up and set up a wallet, including multisig (a simple slider picks 2 to 15 keys). One HTML file, no dependencies, no network.

This is the only place to download it. For real funds, use that downloaded file on an offline computer.</p>

## Quick start

1. Download `voidforge.html`, `SHA256SUMS` and `SHA256SUMS.asc` from the [latest release](https://github.com/Random21m/VOIDFORGE/releases/latest) and check them with tools that are not the page: `gpg --verify SHA256SUMS.asc SHA256SUMS` (import the release key first; its fingerprint and steps are in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)), then `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`. Releases up to v2.8.0 are not signed; check those with `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS` only.
2. Copy it to a USB stick and open it in a desktop browser (Safari 14+, Chrome or Firefox) on an **offline computer**. Phones: for learning and practice only, never for real funds (the page works in a phone browser once you open it from a real file, not a preview, or from the [online practice copy](https://random21m.github.io/VOIDFORGE/)); for real funds use an offline computer.
3. Confirm the green **SELF-TEST PASSED (155/155)** badge and the **NETWORK: OFFLINE** tag. A red notice saying the script is not running means the page did not start; do not use it.
4. Pick Dice, Coin, Word draw, Cards & hex or Damascus (dice and cards folded together by hand), choose 12, 15, 18, 21 or 24 words (12 or 24 if a SeedSigner, Coldcard or Keystone must make the same words from your dice), enter your physical rolls, flips or cards (type, paste, or tap the buttons; the minimum or more, and every one counts) and watch the gray preview words change with every roll, then press FORGE SEED. The words appear as a numbered grid. A passphrase, if you want one, goes in the box right under the words ("Passphrase and note"); changing it does not clear your words, it only works out the fingerprint and addresses again.
5. Write the words down, then compare the words and the master fingerprint with a second independent offline tool before trusting real funds.

**Layout:** one narrow column, the same on a phone and a desktop. The header has the golden die and the name VOIDFORGE on the left, with DOWNLOAD FOR USB and a MENU on the right (GUIDE ME, TEXT A+, PRINT SEED, PRINT STAMP SHEET, IS ANYTHING WATCHING THIS PAGE?, the light/dark switch and, last and in red, END SESSION live in the MENU). Under the header, one start row holds the starting choices: the "New here?" pointer to GUIDE ME, I FOUND A BITCOIN BACKUP (the one gold button) and OPEN PRACTICE SIGNER (gray, for the practice signer and its lessons). A hairline runs across the page under the header, and four file-style tabs sit below it: MAKE A SEED, CHECK A BACKUP, EXTRA TOOLS and ABOUT & HELP. Inside MAKE A SEED a step bar shows where you are (choose, roll, forge, passphrase, check), a short line under it says what the step is for and why, five tabs pick how you make the seed (Dice, Coin, Word draw, Cards & hex or Damascus), the roll counter is red until you have enough rolls or flips and then green with a tick, and your seed words appear right below the FORGE SEED button, inside their boxes at every screen width. The page opens in charcoal; the menu switches to white. Red is used only for things that warn or wipe.

**How this differs from other offline tools.** Tools such as Ian Coleman's BIP39 page and bitaddress.org are well known and useful, and VOIDFORGE is not a replacement for them. The difference is the focus: VOIDFORGE is built for one job, making a wallet seed from dice or coins you roll yourself, with the words and the checks explained in plain language. Its entropy is only the rolls or flips you type in (never mouse movement, timing or the browser's random generator), and it has no paper-wallet or single-key mode. Using a second tool to compare your words and fingerprint is still a good idea, and the page tells you to.

**Fairness test and Word finder (v1.25.1):** under the roll chart, once you have 30 rolls (20 flips) the page shows a Pearson chi-square number and p-value for the face counts, with a plain-language reading. It can show that counts look uneven; it can never prove your dice or coin are fair. In the Word draw box, a Word finder turns a word number (1 to 2048), a D8 roll plus two hex dice, or (v2.3.0) a BitBox02 diceware code such as `2143 2 T` into the BIP39 word, so dice-to-word methods can be followed by hand; ADD WORD (or Enter) puts it at the end of your words.

**BIP85 outputs other than seed words are advanced and secret.** The Child seed tool also makes a Bitcoin Core HD seed (WIF), a master xprv, raw hex, Base64 and Base85 passwords and dice rolls, from the same 64 bytes of BIP85 entropy. They are live secrets: each one is shown on screen only, with no copy or save (since v2.4.0 a print button prints it on one sheet, after asking), and is matched against the published BIP85 test vectors and independent code.

**Not checkable here: signer anti-exfil (sign-to-contract).** Hardware wallets such as Jade can prove their signing nonce was not chosen to leak your key, but the proof needs the random data the computer sent and the signer's first nonce commitment. Those exist only during the live exchange and are not stored in a PSBT or transaction, so VOIDFORGE cannot check them afterwards. The signature checks it does perform (nonce reuse, sighash types) work on the finished transaction only.

**Browser testing:** automated tests run in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (`tests/browsers.test.mjs`; the full suite runs in Chromium). v1.7.0, v1.8.0, v1.8.1, v1.9.0, v1.10.0, v1.10.1 and v1.11.0 were checked by hand in Firefox and Safari on a desktop computer (self-test badge, dice buttons, watch-only box, QR codes and, in v1.8.0, the fingerprint picture all worked; v1.5.0 also matched the published dice example). v1.7.1 only added disclaimer text, and v1.8.1 only changed one message and the release process. v1.9.0 added the roll chart. v1.10.0 added the five-address list, the 4-letter view and the check guide. The watch-only QR was scanned into Blue Wallet on a phone and worked (same first receive address as VOIDFORGE; a zpub import shows no master fingerprint by design, while the descriptor does). The descriptors were imported into Sparrow Wallet, and the fingerprint picture matched what Sparrow shows for four wallets. The maintainer reports that later releases, up to v1.18.0, also work in Firefox and Safari on a desktop computer (reported by hand, without a per-version checklist). Electrum and Nunchuk have not been tested. Please report anything odd in other browsers. The page needs Safari 14 or newer. Phones: for learning and practice only, never for real funds.

**Coin flips:** decide which side is 1 and which is 0 (the usual choice is heads = 1, tails = 0, and the two tap buttons are drawn as gold coins that way), use it for every flip, and keep the exact 1/0 string with your backup. Only the string is hashed, so any consistent choice works, but the mapping cannot be recovered from the words.

- **Restore drill:** after FORGE, a card in the Check step hides the words on screen and has you type them back from your paper. It reports which word positions are wrong (never what the right word is), checks the passphrase if there is one, and shows the master fingerprint your paper rebuilds. Changing or clearing the seed ends the drill, and nothing is stored.
- **First 10 receive and 10 change addresses and a check guide:** after FORGE the first ten receive and first ten change addresses are listed (with COPY ALL 10 and COPY ALL 10 CHANGE) and a short guide explains how to compare them in a second wallet app. Comparing twenty addresses is much stronger than comparing one.
- **First 4 letters view:** a button on the word grid shows only the first four letters of each word (every BIP39 word is unique by them), for stamping metal backups. Display only; it does not change the stored words.
- **Roll chart:** a small bar chart under the roll box shows how many times each face (or coin side) has come up. It is for your eyes only and does not change the seed or the randomness check, which still cannot prove your dice or coin are fair.
- **Taproot addresses:** the first ten Taproot (BIP86, `bc1p...`) receive and change addresses are also listed, for checking in wallets that use Taproot. The zpub, descriptors and QR codes still describe the SegWit wallet.
- **Printable backup sheet:** PRINT SEED gives a two-column numbered sheet with the fingerprint, first address and space for notes (the passphrase is never printed).
- **Bitcoin dictionary:** a searchable, offline glossary of 366 Bitcoin terms (in v2.16.0) in plain words (keys and seeds, wallets, transactions, mining, Lightning, privacy and more), with topic buttons. It is updated with every release, and where a term appears in VOIDFORGE the entry says where.
- **Check your download:** pick the downloaded file and the page works out its SHA-256 and content ID offline, then compares them with the published ones you paste. A page cannot vouch for itself, so for real money also check with `sha256sum` (the page says so).
- **Compare a key or descriptor:** in CHECK A BACKUP the backup box accepts a zpub/xpub, a wpkh descriptor or a Taproot address from a wallet app and says MATCH or NO MATCH against your words (public keys only; it cannot show the words).
- **Explain an address, or check that it is yours:** paste an address (bc1q, bc1p, 3... or 1...); VOIDFORGE searches the receive and change chains of your words (and passphrase) and tells you the address number. Searches 50 per chain per press.
- **SeedQR input:** the words box in CHECK A BACKUP also reads a SeedSigner SeedQR digit string or CompactSeedQR hex and shows the words (typed in, or scanned with SCAN SEEDQR on the offline computer).
- **Child seed (BIP85):** derives a new seed phrase from your parent words and passphrase (12, 18 or 24 words, any index) for a separate wallet, or any other BIP85 output: a Bitcoin Core HD seed (WIF), a master xprv, hex bytes, a Base64 or Base85 password, or dice rolls. Shown on screen, with a print button (v2.4.0) and no copy or save. The parent, the type, its settings and the index recreate it; each output matches the published BIP85 test vectors.
- **Fingerprint picture:** a small LifeHash picture next to the master fingerprint (the same one Sparrow Wallet shows), for a quick visual match.
- **Reforge (seed recovery):** in EXTRA TOOLS. *Missing or wrong words:* type your words with `?` for any you lack, and Reforge fills in one or two unknown words, finds one wrong word or two swapped words, or puts back a word missing from an unknown place. *Passphrase:* type your words and your passphrase guesses or pieces, and it tries them alone and joined, in four letter cases, optionally with one typo. Every candidate is checked against something public you still have from the wallet (its master fingerprint, an address, or its account xpub/zpub), and it reports only what matched, says plainly when nothing did, and says how likely a fingerprint-only match is to be a coincidence. Typing 11 words and `?` lists the possible last words (this replaces Find the last word). What you type is secret: it stays in the page and CLEAR wipes it. A browser can only do small searches; for bigger ones, open-source tools such as BTCRecover run on a computer you trust.
- **Transaction (PSBT) inspector:** paste or choose a partly signed Bitcoin transaction (or a raw one) to see its inputs, outputs, addresses, fee, whether it can be replaced (RBF), any locktime and any data note before you sign it on a hardware wallet. It also reads any signatures already in it and flags a **repeated nonce** (two different signatures sharing a secret number, which would let anyone work out the private key) and any **sighash type other than ALL** (which lets others change the transaction after it is signed). It ends with a list of checks and a verdict, and says **NO CLEAN VERDICT** whenever it cannot fully check something. Read only: it never signs and never uses your seed.
- **Compare two transactions:** paste what you sent to your signer and what came back, or an original and its fee-bump replacement. It shows every difference in plain words, tells you whether the signer changed anything, and whether a replacement is a plain fee bump (same payments, more fee). It also checks for a repeated nonce across the two transactions. Read only.
- **Testnet and multisig cosigner keys:** a Network menu switches the watch-only export and the address finder to testnet, and the Multisig cosigner types give the BIP48 key and origin for a multisig wallet (Sparrow, Specter, Nunchuk, Electrum). Public keys only.
- **Address types and accounts:** the watch-only export can be switched to Taproot, Nested SegWit (ypub) or Legacy, with any account number, and *Explain an address, or check that it is yours* finds bc1q, bc1p, 3... and 1... addresses for the chosen account. Since v2.8.1 the box shows the first ten receive and first ten change addresses for the chosen type, account and network (five receive addresses from v1.26.0), with COPY ALL 10, so you can compare them in your wallet app. Your 12 or 24 words do not depend on the address type: the type is chosen in the wallet app, so write it down (and the account number) with your backup.
- **Paper backup as a SeedQR:** after FORGE (12 or 24 words), SHOW SEEDQR or SHOW COMPACTSEEDQR displays the code on screen. COPY BY HAND walks through it in 7x7 blocks with row/column numbers and a mini-map so you can copy it with a pen or pencil onto grid paper, like SeedSigner. You can also print the QR, the guide or a blank grid (printing asks for confirmation, since it sends your seed to a printer). Verify a copy by scanning it into a SeedSigner and comparing the fingerprint.
- **How your seed was made:** after FORGE, an optional SECRET-marked view walks through five steps from your rolls to the words (SHA-256, raw entropy, checksum, 11-bit pieces, word numbers) and checks itself against the real words.
- **Printed backup sheet:** PRINT SEED makes a clean sheet with numbered boxes for the words, the fingerprint, first address and fields to fill in (never the passphrase).
- **Browser safety checklist:** in ABOUT & HELP, eight short habits with the reason for each (go offline first, a clean browser window, translation, clipboard history, print and PDF copies, downloads, restarting afterwards).

The passphrase help also says that some hardware wallets limit how long a passphrase can be or which characters it can use, so test a small amount on your device first.
- **Stale results (v1.26.1, v1.29.1):** if you change the rolls, word count, dice/coin choice or passphrase after FORGE, the old words, fingerprint and address are cleared and you are asked to press FORGE SEED again, and DOWNLOAD saves the rolls that made the words. Since v1.29.1 a FORGE that is still working out its fingerprint and addresses is also stopped by any such change or by CLEAR ALL, so it cannot write them back late.

## Real signing with the practice signer (v2.17.0)

The practice signer signs your own transactions and messages for real. Read this first:

- **Which amounts.** Real signing here is for learning, amounts you could afford to lose, and getting coins out when your usual signer is lost or broken. For savings, sign with a hardware signer or Bitcoin Core on a dedicated offline computer. THREAT_MODEL.md says why, and what VOIDFORGE does and does not prove.
- **A browser is not a hardware signer.** A real SeedSigner keeps your seed off every computer. Here the seed is in a browser tab, where malware, an extension or a screen recorder could copy it. For real coins use it only on an offline computer you trust, then CLEAR ALL and restart the computer.
- **Check every address and amount on the device's screen** against what you meant to send. Single-key change is shown as change only when the device makes it again from your seed (its derivation path); anything it cannot make is shown as a payment, as on SeedSigner. **Multisig change is checked only against a wallet descriptor of yours scanned on the device.** Without one, SeedSigner offers Skip verification and shows what the PSBT claims: the page calls that change "claimed change, NOT verified" in red, and before you sign it asks a second question that names the amount. The page also checks that a scanned descriptor holds your seed's own key at the path it names, and counts it only if it also makes the coins being spent. A descriptor stays loaded until the device is switched off or another is scanned (SeedSigner 0.8.7 forgets it at Home).
- **Refused on the device:** a PSBT for another network than the device's, and a PSBT whose full previous transaction for an input is not the coin it spends (its txid does not match) or disagrees with the input's witness UTXO. Such a PSBT can claim any amount for the coin and so hide a fee of almost everything; SeedSigner 0.8.7 does not check this. The device says why on its screen.
- **Before a real seed signs, the page asks** you to confirm the outputs are checked and the computer is offline; while it is online the question says so plainly. A message signed with a real seed is asked about too, offline or online. No signs nothing. With nothing the seed can sign, there is no question and the page says "Nothing signed".
- **Warnings before the scan:** a PSBT that no seed on the device can sign, inputs of other signers, inputs the seed will leave unsigned (sighash types SeedSigner does not sign; a non-Taproot input that asks for sighash 0 is signed with SIGHASH_ALL, as SeedSigner does), a fee higher than the payment or above 1,000 sat/vB (red), a fee of 10% of the amount sent or above 250 sat/vB (amber), change at an odd path (another account than the inputs', an unusual depth, an address number past 10,000), outputs that name your seed but are not provably yours, and a Testnet address on a Mainnet device. A PSBT the device cannot read is not held up.
- **The signed PSBT is offered only when it is right:** its transaction must be the one you held up (worked out by the page's own code; the sequence of every input is kept exactly, where SeedSigner 0.8.7 turns a sequence of 0 into 0xFFFFFFFF and so signs a different transaction) and every signature must check out with the page's own code. Otherwise the page says so in red and offers nothing to copy or save. A partly signed result says which inputs were signed and which were left.
- **SegWit v0 amounts.** For a SegWit v0 input without its full previous transaction, the amount comes from the PSBT's witness UTXO alone, as on SeedSigner 0.8.7 and on this page. When a PSBT with more than one input leaves out a SegWit v0 input's full previous transaction, the page shows an amber note. A dishonest wallet can then ask you to sign the same transaction twice, each time lowering a different input's amount, and combine the two signatures into one transaction whose real fee is far larger than either review showed (the SegWit v0 "two-pass" fee attack). Be wary of a wallet that asks you to sign the same transaction again. Taproot signatures commit to every input's amount, so it does not work there. Use a wallet you trust, and prefer PSBTs that include the full previous transactions (the page then checks them, above).
- **Seeds and keys are kept out of the box, in their common forms (a safety net, not a guarantee).** Seed words (numbered, with commas or "and", in a sentence, run together, the first 4 letters, even with a typo), SLIP-39 share words, SeedQR digits (with or without spaces), hex keys or entropy (also with colons), WIF keys (also as bitcoin:...), BIP38 keys, codex32 shares and xprv keys are refused, nothing of them is repeated, and the box is cleared; the message box gets the same check for a seed with a right checksum. A file is checked before it goes into the box, and the camera never puts a SeedQR or a key there. Re-hide covers the box. Never paste a secret here: the check cannot know every form.
- A public seed (a test phrase, a Damascus practice sheet) signs as **PRACTICE**; a real seed's result says it is real. Re-hide covers the signed output while a real seed is loaded; every wipe clears it.

## The Damascus method

Damascus steel is made by folding two different steels into one blade. The **Damascus method** (MAKE A SEED, the Damascus tab) folds **dice** and **playing cards** into one standard 12- or 24-word seed (BIP39), by hand, with checks on every step. On screen it goes in five steps: Hammer, Anvil, Forge, Words, Last word.

*The Damascus method, created by Random, 10 November 2026.* Dated and signed origin record: tag [damascus-origin-2026-11-10](https://github.com/Random21m/VOIDFORGE/releases/tag/damascus-origin-2026-11-10), published on GitLab (the project's home until its GitLab account became unavailable on 11 November 2026; from v2.15.0 the home is GitHub), timestamped in Bitcoin block 975,337 (OpenTimestamps) ([DAMASCUS-ORIGIN.md](DAMASCUS-ORIGIN.md)).

- The dice fill the **Hammer sheet**, the cards fill the **Anvil sheet**, and folding the two (same bits give 0, different bits give 1) gives the **Forge sheet**. Its rows are looked up in a printed word table. If either the dice or the shuffle is good, the seed is random.
- **Hallmarks** (1 if a row or column has an odd number of 1s) catch copying mistakes, not cheating. They reveal part of the seed (22 bits for 12 words, 34 for 24), so keep them only next to the words.
- The last word needs a computer. A SeedSigner and VOIDFORGE both work it out, and both must agree. Its 7 bits (3 for 24 words) come from the Forge sheet; they are not new coin flips. CALCULATE LAST WORD works it out from the Forge sheet alone.
- The checks catch mistakes and badly broken dice or a lazy shuffle. They cannot prove dice or a shuffle fair, and they do not catch cheating. The method has not been independently audited.
- **Quick path on the page:** enter the dice (step 1) and the cards (step 2) and press FORGE SEED; the page does the Forge, the hallmarks, the words and the last word, as the other MAKE A SEED tabs do. Steps 3 to 5 are the paper path: typing your hallmarks and words there only checks your paper. Using only the page means trusting its math; the paper steps let you check every bit yourself.
- Practice first: **GUIDED PRACTICE** fills in a published practice sheet one part at a time (NEXT), saying where every value comes from and which box of which sheet it goes in; FILL IN A PRACTICE SHEET fills it in at once. Never put money on a practice seed.
- About 30 minutes once you know the steps; allow longer the first time. Field-tested: a hand-made Damascus seed checked on a real SeedSigner and in Sparrow (10 November 2026).

Read: [the quick guide](docs/damascus-quick-guide.md) (the steps), [the full method](docs/damascus-method.md), [questions and fixes](docs/damascus-faq.md), [worked examples](docs/damascus-examples.md) (public: never put money on them) and [the math, simulations and open questions](docs/damascus-method-design.md). Print the sheets from the page, or open the kits: [12 words](docs/damascus-kit.html) and [24 words](docs/damascus-kit-24.html).

## Recommended hardware wallet: SeedSigner

For real funds, VOIDFORGE recommends [SeedSigner](https://seedsigner.com) as the signing device. The reasons come from its own source code (github.com/SeedSigner/seedsigner, release 0.8.7) and, for the SeedSigner+ kit, its seller's description:

- **Open source** (MIT license), so anyone can read exactly what it does.
- **It stores no seed.** Seeds live only in memory and are wiped when it is switched off; your backup stays on your paper or steel.
- **Air-gapped:** it talks to your wallet app only through QR codes, read by its camera and shown on its screen. The SeedSigner+ kit is built on a Raspberry Pi Zero 1.3, which its seller notes is made without Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
- **Dice-made seeds** (50 or 99 rolls) and SeedQR backups, which VOIDFORGE can make and check the same way.

A recommendation is not a guarantee: verify the SeedSigner software you install as its project explains, and keep using VOIDFORGE to cross-check its words, fingerprint and addresses. To learn how it works without risk, try its screens and buttons in the **Practice signer** (CHECK A BACKUP), with its six lessons and its practice transaction; it is for practice only, never for real coins.
