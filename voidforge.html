MAKE A SEED

Pick 12 or 24 words, enter your dice rolls or coin flips, then press FORGE SEED and write the words on paper.

Step 1 of 5

Choose how your seed is made

Your wallet starts as a very large random number. Real dice (or a coin) are how you create it yourself, instead of trusting a computer to pick it. First choose the length: 12 words is strong enough for almost everyone and needs about 50 dice rolls. 24 words is the longest option and needs about 99 rolls. Then choose dice (easier for most people) or coin (many more flips). If you have physical word tiles, use the Word draw box instead. Making real keys? Read BEFORE YOU MAKE REAL KEYS just above this card first.

Roll your dice

Roll a real die, then tap the number it shows. Do not make numbers up: people are bad at being random, and the whole point is that nobody could guess your rolls. You need 50 rolls. The bar fills as you go, and NEXT unlocks when you have enough. The grey words below change with every roll, so you can see how one roll changes every word. Make sure nobody can see your screen.

Forge the seed and write it down

Press FORGE SEED. Your rolls are scrambled together into your seed words. (A passphrase, if you want one, comes next, after you can see the words.) Write the words on paper, in order, with a pen. Never take a photo, type them into a connected device, or store them in a cloud. Anyone who has these words has your money. NEXT unlocks once the words are shown.

Optional: add a passphrase

A passphrase is an extra secret that makes a different wallet from the same words. It protects you if someone finds your paper, but if you lose it the funds are gone for good. If you are not sure, skip this step. If you want one, type it twice in the Passphrase box under your words. The page then shows the fingerprint without the passphrase and with it, so you can see they are different wallets. Write the passphrase down separately from the words.

Check it and make a backup

Last step, and the most important. Compare the fingerprint and first address in a second wallet app, make a paper backup if you like (SeedQR or the printed sheet), then press START DRILL in the Restore drill below: it hides the words on screen and has you type them back from your paper. (The CHECK A BACKUP tab can also check words you already have.) Start with a small amount first.

Word draw: made your words with physical tiles?SECRETType words drawn from word tiles (such as SeedSigner’s SeedPills) plus a few coin flips.
How Word draw works

Word tiles let you pick the words by hand: mix them in a bag, draw one without looking, flip it to choose a face, write the word down and put the tile back before the next draw. Each word is 11 random bits. A 12-word seed needs 11 drawn words and 7 coin flips; a 24-word seed needs 23 words and 3 flips. The flips are needed because part of the last word is a checksum. Type your words (the first four letters of each are enough) and your flips; VOIDFORGE finishes the last word the standard BIP39 way, with no hashing, so any BIP39 wallet reads the result. Repeated words are fine, because every draw goes back in the bag. It never fills in the flips for you. Typed words are secret: use it only where nobody can see your screen.

Using dice instead of tiles?
Type a word number (1–2048), or a D8 roll followed by two 16-sided dice (0–F), written with a space between them, such as 3 A 7 (a plain number with no spaces is a word number). The word number is (D8 − 1) × 256 + the two hex digits + 1. For the final word of a 24-word seed, a last D8 roll gives 3 bits: write it as flips (1 = 000, 2 = 001 ... 8 = 111).
The passphrase (if any) is added after the words are made, in the box under them. Coin: pick which side is 1 and keep it the same for every flip.
Pick which side is 1 and which is 0 (the usual choice: heads = 1, tails = 0) and keep it the same for every flip. Only the 1/0 string is used, so any consistent choice is fine, but you cannot get the mapping back from the words. Write down the exact string or your mapping with your backup.

          Reforge: recover a seed or find a passphraseSECRETMissing or wrong words, or a forgotten passphrase — search against what you already know.

          Reforge searches for the words or passphrase you lost, checked against something you know is right: your wallet’s master fingerprint, one of its addresses, or its account xpub/zpub (all three are public, and a watch-only wallet shows them). It never guesses: it reports only what matched, and tells you plainly when nothing did. What you type here is secret; it is never saved or sent, and CLEAR wipes it.

          Type your words in order, with ? for each word you do not have. Reforge picks the search from what you type: with one or two ? it tries every word in those places; with all the words it tries every swap of two words and every single word replaced; with one word fewer (11, 14, 17, 20 or 23) it tries the missing word at every place. A word that is not on the BIP39 list counts as ?, and the first 4 letters of a word are enough. To list the words that could be last, type all but the last word, then ?. Two ? on a 12-word phrase can take a long time; an account xpub/zpub is by far the fastest thing to check against (a fingerprint or an address is several times slower), the page shows progress, and STOP ends the search.

          For a wallet whose words you have but whose passphrase you do not remember exactly. Type your seed words, then your guesses or the pieces you remember, one per line, exactly as you might have typed them (spaces count). Reforge tries no passphrase, each line, and every two or three lines joined (with or without a space, in any order), each as typed, in lowercase, in UPPERCASE and Capitalised; tick one typo to also try each with a character missing, doubled, swapped with the next one, or in the other case. Each try takes the browser about a millisecond against an xpub/zpub and several times longer against a fingerprint or address, so the page counts the tries first and refuses more than 1,000,000.

            ⚠ BEFORE YOU MAKE REAL KEYS
            

            Three ways a computer can quietly keep your secrets, and the simple fix for each.
            

            1
            Your computer may keep a copy of your keys. It can write memory to the hard drive (swap files, hibernation) without asking, and the browser cannot stop it.What to do: for real money, use an offline computer with an encrypted drive, and turn hibernation off.
            

            2
            Copying can leak. Clipboard history can keep what you copy or paste, and may sync it to your other devices.What to do: write seeds on paper; avoid copy and paste for anything secret, and turn clipboard history off.
            

            3
            Keys can stay in memory after you finish. Wiping the page is a precaution, not a guarantee.What to do: when you are done, close the browser and restart the computer.

      How multisig works, and what it costsSeveral keys, any M of them can spend. Read this before you set one up.
      
            A multisig wallet is built from N separate seeds and spends only when M of them agree. With 2-of-3 you can lose one seed, or have one stolen, and still be safe. It is the standard way to remove the “one piece of paper” risk.
            
      
              
        
            • Back up the descriptor too. The seeds alone are not enough: to find the money you also need the list of all the public keys (the descriptor, or the printed recovery sheet). It contains no secrets, so keep copies in several places.
              • 
        
            • Keep every seed in a different place, and make each one on a different day or device if you can.
              • 
        
            • Never reuse a seed as two of the keys, or the same seed in two different multisig wallets of the same account.
              • 
        
            • Test with a small amount first, and practice recovering with M of the seeds before you trust it with real savings.
              • 
        
            • Spending needs a wallet app (Sparrow, Nunchuk, Bitcoin Core) plus signers. VOIDFORGE only sets the wallet up; it never signs.

            The roll or flip string is hashed as plain text. Dice are digits 1–6; coin flips are the characters 1 and 0 (choose which side counts as 1 and keep the exact string). This is the same method SeedSigner uses for its 12/24-word dice and coin-flip modes, and VOIDFORGE reproduces SeedSigner’s published dice examples. Coldcard uses the same dice method (SHA-256 of the roll digits as plain text; 24 words, or the first half of the hash for 12), and VOIDFORGE reproduces the worked example in Coldcard’s documentation: the rolls 123456 give mirror reject rookie … garment (24 words). That example is too short to be safe; it only proves the method matches. Only 12- and 24-word seeds are generated, so every seed can be reproduced by SeedSigner-style tools. The VERIFY box still accepts 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24-word phrases from other tools.
            
    
            
    
            
      Master fingerprint & first address
      
            PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512(mnemonic, "mnemonic"+passphrase, 2048) → seed
            
         HMAC-SHA512("Bitcoin seed", seed) → master private key
            
         secp256k1 pubkey → RIPEMD160(SHA256(pubkey)) → first 4 bytes
            
      
            Fingerprint picture. Next to the master fingerprint is a small coloured picture (a LifeHash, version 2, made from SHA-256 of the fingerprint). Sparrow Wallet shows the same picture for the same fingerprint, so a matching picture is a quick visual sign that you are looking at the same wallet. It is only a convenience: the 8-character fingerprint and the first address are the real checks, and the picture does not depend on your keys beyond the fingerprint.
            
      
            Watch-only export. After FORGE, the box “Watch-only wallet” shows the public key of the account m/84’/0’/0’ as a zpub (Electrum, Blue Wallet, Sparrow) and as output descriptors wpkh([fingerprint/84h/0h/0h]xpub…/0/*) for receiving and /1/* for change, with the descriptor checksum (Sparrow, Nunchuk, Bitcoin Core). The Network menu switches to testnet (test coins only; keys start with tpub, upub or vpub). The two Multisig cosigner choices give the BIP48 key (Zpub or Ypub) plus its origin, to add as one cosigner in a multisig wallet. The Address type menu switches it to Taproot (tr, BIP86), Nested SegWit (sh(wpkh), BIP49, ypub) or Legacy (pkh, BIP44), and the Account box changes the account number (almost always 0). It holds no private keys and cannot spend, but whoever has it can see every address and balance of that wallet, so give it only to your own wallet app. It is specific to the passphrase you entered. The SHOW QR buttons draw the same data as a QR code on screen so another device can scan it (many apps accept a scanned zpub or descriptor; if yours does not, use COPY). After importing, check that the app’s first receive address equals the First receive address shown here.
            
      
            First receive address. Also shown is the first Bitcoin mainnet receive address of a standard native SegWit (BIP84) wallet: BIP32 child keys along m/84’/0’/0’/0/0, then RIPEMD160(SHA256(pubkey)) encoded as a bc1q… bech32 address. Use it to cross-check against a wallet that imports the same words (a watch-only wallet is enough): the fingerprint alone is a weak check, a matching first address is a strong one. It is only correct for wallets that use the BIP84 path; other wallet types (legacy, nested SegWit, Taproot) show different addresses. It includes the passphrase if you entered one; VERIFY shows it without a passphrase and, if you type one into its own passphrase box, with it too.
            
      
            RIPEMD-160 and secp256k1 are implemented in this file (no libraries). The curve code is not constant-time, which is fine offline but another reason never to run this on a networked or shared computer. The fingerprint includes the optional BIP39 passphrase; VERIFY shows it without a passphrase and, if you type one into its own passphrase box, with it too.
            
    
            
    
            
      What SELF-TEST checks
      
            It runs automatically every time the page loads; if any test fails, seed generation is disabled and a red banner appears. Press SELF-TEST to see the full list. It checks: the BIP39 wordlist hash; official BIP39 entropy vectors (12 to 24 words); SeedSigner’s published 50- and 99-roll dice examples and 50-roll fingerprint; coin-flip vectors from an independent reference implementation of SeedSigner’s method; the abandon…about fingerprints (with and without the TREZOR passphrase); BIP32 test vector 1; the first receive address (the published BIP84 vector, a second vector from two independent libraries, and the BIP173 bech32 example); the watch-only zpub (published BIP84 vector and an independent-library vector), the descriptor checksum, a QR code matrix checked against an independent QR library, and the fingerprint picture checked against independent LifeHash code and Sparrow Wallet; passphrase normalization; RIPEMD-160 vectors; and the phrase checker. If any test fails, do not use that copy for real funds.
            
      
            What the self-test cannot catch: it only runs known answers through this same file, so it can miss a bug on inputs it does not cover, and a tampered file could simply fake a pass. It does not replace checking the file’s SHA256 against the release, and it does not replace comparing your words and fingerprint in a second independent tool. The network tag at the top reads the browser’s online flag only; it cannot prove the machine is air-gapped.
            
    
            
    
            
      Security notes
      
              
        
            • Only use this offline, on a computer. Never type a real seed into a phone, website or online tool; phones and file previews often will not run this page at all.
              • 
        
            • The randomness meter checks face counts, long runs, repeating or stepping patterns and periodic repeats. It is advisory only: it catches lazy input, not subtle bias, and cannot prove your dice or coin are good. If it flags a pattern, FORGE SEED asks before continuing.
              • 
        
            • A Content-Security-Policy built into this file tells the browser to block all network access, as a second line of defense. It does not replace using an offline computer.
              • 
        
            • This page contains no links, and switching between dice and coin clears the entered rolls (after asking).
              • 
        
            • The red "appears to be online" banner is a hint from the browser, not proof. A machine can be connected without the browser knowing, so physically disconnect it.
              • 
        
            • Text boxes turn off spellcheck, autofill and autocorrect so the browser doesn’t keep what you type. The passphrase is masked, and is never shown with, copied with, printed with or saved with the words.
              • 
        
            • There is deliberately no "copy seed" button. SEND TO VERIFY moves the words to the VERIFY box without the clipboard, but that only re-runs the same code. For a real check, write the words down by hand and type them into a second tool. The clipboard is where seeds most often leak. PRINT SEED and DOWNLOAD ask for confirmation because the print spooler and your disk can retain the seed.
              • 
        
            • DOWNLOAD FOR USB saves a clean copy of this page and never includes a generated seed. It is re-serialized by the browser, so its file hash will not match the original; to verify a hash, copy the original file instead. DOWNLOAD (seed) writes your seed and rolls to a plain-text file, so only use it on a trusted offline machine.

You alone are responsible for your funds, your backups and how you use it. If you lose the words or the passphrase, nobody can recover the funds. Before trusting it, check the release hash, use an offline computer, restore the wallet from your written backup in a second tool, and move a small amount first.             Before you make real keys
            

            VOIDFORGE is an educational tool. It works fully offline and never stores anything, and its code is open to read, but it has not been independently audited. Your computer, though, can keep secrets. Three things worth knowing first:
            

            1
            Your computer may keep a copy of your keys. It can write memory to the hard drive (swap files, hibernation) without asking, and the browser cannot stop it.What to do: for real money, use an offline computer with an encrypted drive, and turn hibernation off.
            

            2
            Copying can leak. Clipboard history can keep what you copy or paste, and may sync it to your other devices.What to do: write seeds on paper; avoid copy and paste for anything secret, and turn clipboard history off.
            

            3
            Keys can stay in memory after you finish. Wiping the page is a precaution, not a guarantee.What to do: when you are done, close the browser and restart the computer.
